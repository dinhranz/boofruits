General

A grape is a fruit, botanically a berry, of the deciduous woody vines of the flowering plant genus Vitis. Grapes are a non-climacteric type of fruit, generally occurring in clusters.

History

Grape cultivation originated in the Middle East approximately 6,000 to 8,000 years ago, where the natural presence of yeast on grape skins led to the early discovery of winemaking.

Archaeological findings suggest that wine culture first flourished in Georgia 8,000 years ago, while the oldest known winery, dating back to 4000 BC, was discovered in Armenia. As the practice spread, the Persian city of Shiraz became renowned for its high-quality production, eventually lending its name to the famous Syrah grape variety. Throughout history, ancient civilizations such as the Egyptians, Greeks, and Romans expanded grape growing across Europe and North Africa, while Cyprus established a lasting legacy with Commandaria, the world’s oldest manufactured wine still in production.

In North America, although native grapes grew wild for centuries, it was not until the 19th-century creation of the Concord grape by Ephraim Bull that the fruit became a major agricultural staple in the region.

Nutrition

Raw grapes are 81% water, 18% carbohydrates, 1% protein, and have negligible fat (table). A 100-gram (31⁄2-ounce) reference amount of raw grapes supplies 288 kilojoules (69 kilocalories) of food energy and a moderate amount of vitamin K (12% of the Daily Value), with no other micronutrients in significant amounts (table).
